**Experiment 17: Deep Dive into Transformer Depth and Batch Size**

Okay, so after digging deep into Experiment 16, I'm thinking we've got some serious performance headroom left, and I'm ready to pounce. Exp 16, while a win, still has some clear bottlenecks. Specifically, that 181ms per step time is killing me. And I'm seeing a ton of waste in the optimizer and the n-gram setup.

My current train of thought is to go for the biggest, cleanest win first. It's time to test **scaling Transformer depth**. Everything else is staying the same, the point is to isolate the effect of depth. I'm going to set `DEPTH` to 12. And to make sure we have enough VRAM, I'll set `DEVICE_BATCH_SIZE` to 64 and use gradient accumulation to keep the effective batch size the same. This lets us evaluate pure architectural scaling with minimal noise.

My reasoning? The dense transformer is tiny compared to the n-gram tables. Increasing depth from 8 to 12 layers should be a HUGE boost to its capacity and ability to do contextual reasoning, especially with the 1.61B n-gram params handling local memorization. Chinchilla scaling says a +50% increase in params (due to depth) for a 20% loss of tokens due to the fixed per-step overhead, is net positive. Deep is the way to go.
Also, the current `window_pattern = "SSSL"` works great with `DEPTH = 12`. It’s all aligned.

The only downsides are that the total number of tokens for the run decreases slightly, but it’s still more than enough tokens for the size and complexity of the model.

If Exp 17 wins, we can then dive into other dimensions, like making the n-gram system faster and improving those HBM traffic bottlenecks.
